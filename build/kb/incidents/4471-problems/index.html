How the reports were told apart

Every report was merged on its subject line (decision 0001), and the subjects say nothing about the cause: each problem shows up under several subjects, and the same subject turns up under more than one problem. So the split goes by what the body says and where the report came from, never by the subject.

  • A swap or a shift? Read the two dates in the body. Booked 7 April, invite on 4 July is a swap: the day and the month changed places. Invite on Tuesday 12 May, calendar on Monday 11 May is a shift: one day, no swap. The desk had already marked the shifts (oneDayMove in desk-notes.json); they get their own row and ticket here, not a footnote, and stay counted under their tenant’s cause, as the desk’s own table counts them.
  • Which swap? It depends on the tenant, not the reporter’s browser (causes.ts). Every tenant on file writes the day first. A report sent through a booking-v3 form went through a form that prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field: that is hint. Everything else hit the shared parser: that is parser.
  • Count each report once. The export repeats some rows; one id is one report.

The tag intake put on a report is a guess from the reporter’s browser. It disagrees with the problem often enough that a triage step should not lean on it.

Where each fix goes

  • parser: the queued fix. Parse in the tenant’s locale, not month first.
  • hint: the booking-v3 date field. Print the tenant’s own format under it, or read what the hint asks for. The parser fix does not touch this path.
  • shift: wherever the invite date is turned into a time zone. A one-day move before or after the parser fix means this one is still open.

For the desk

Merging by subject put three problems in one ticket and marked them known. Until the triage rule changes, when a big ticket is marked known, check its cause table before you close new reports into it: a second row means a second ticket.
